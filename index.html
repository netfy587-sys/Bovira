How to study well

Click. Discover. Enjoy. Four steps that match what research on learning commonly recommends.

Set a small goal

Choose one clear thing to learn in this session, such as ten new words or one concept.

Read actively

Pause often, put ideas in your own words and connect them to what you already know.

Test yourself

Close the book and try to recall the key points before checking your notes.

Review over time

Return to the material after a day, then after a few days, then after a week or more.

Article · 6 min

How memory works

Memory is often described in three stages: taking information in, storing it, and bringing it back when needed. A weakness at any stage can make us feel that we have forgotten something.

Working memory, the space where we hold information for a few moments, is small. We can keep only a handful of items in mind at once, which is why breaking material into small chunks helps.

Article · 5 min

Why we forget, and how reviewing helps

In 1885 the psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus published experiments showing that newly learned material fades quickly if it is not reviewed. His results are often called the forgetting curve.

Later research found that spreading reviews over several days or weeks, known as spaced repetition, usually leads to stronger long-term memory than studying everything in one sitting.

Article · 5 min

Testing yourself beats rereading

Many studies show that trying to recall information from memory strengthens it more than reading it again. This is called retrieval practice, or the testing effect.

Flashcards, short quizzes and explaining a topic aloud without notes are simple ways to use it. Mistakes during practice are useful, because they show what still needs attention.

Article · 5 min

How long does it take to form a habit?

A popular belief says that a habit takes 21 days to form. Research does not support a fixed number. A 2009 study from University College London found that the time varied widely, from about 18 to 254 days, with an average of around 66 days.

The practical lesson is to start small, repeat the action in the same setting, and be patient with slow progress.

Article · 4 min

Sleep and learning

Sleep serves an important role in memory. During sleep the brain helps to consolidate what was learned during the day, so regular, sufficient sleep supports learning more than late-night cramming does.

Learning terms, explained

Words you will meet often, in simple language.

Retrieval practice
Recalling information from memory instead of looking at it again.
Spaced repetition
Reviewing material at increasing intervals over time.
Working memory
The small mental space used to hold and use information for a short time.
Interleaving
Mixing different topics or problem types within one study session.
Consolidation
The process by which a new memory becomes more stable.
Metacognition
Thinking about your own thinking, such as judging how well you know a topic.
